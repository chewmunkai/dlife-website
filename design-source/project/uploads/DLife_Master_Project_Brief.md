# 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH BRIEF

D'Life is a 27-year-old Malaysian financial advisory and insurance agency founded by two sisters, Sharon and Rachel Cheang. We are building their website — starting with the homepage. The brand's entire differentiator is that it rejects the hard-sell culture the insurance industry is known for. Our job is to take the founder's raw, generous, everything-at-once material and refine it into something warm, calm and human that earns trust rather than chases conversions.

**If you remember one thing:** *warmth over persuasion.*

DVA for leaders, Youth Community for young people.

# 5. BRAND POSITION

**Master line:** Real Support. Beyond the Policy.

**The insight:** in insurance, support usually stops the moment the policy is signed. D'Life's promise is that it continues.

**The website's role:** D'Life's **Digital Trust Base**. It confirms the organisation is real, established and human — for people discovering D'Life for the first time *and* for people returning after meeting an advisor offline.

### It should
✓ Confirm D'Life is real, established and human
✓ Help visitors recognise a relevant path quickly
✓ Show proof through people, stories and useful content
✓ Encourage low-pressure human contact

### It should never become
✗ A generic insurance template
✗ A fear-based or hard-selling funnel
✗ An MLM-style recruitment site
✗ A cold corporate or motivational-seminar identity

---

# 6. TONE OF VOICE

**Six words:** Warm · Mature · Established · Professional · Premium but grounded · Human

**The governing principle:** real before inspirational.

### Never write
- Hard-sell or fear-led copy ("what if you're not covered?")
- Generic corporate language
- Exaggerated claims or luxury posturing
- MLM or recruitment hype ("be your own boss", income claims)
- Childish, cartoon or spa-like styling
- Long text walls and random bolding

### The test
If a sentence could appear on any other insurance website in Malaysia, rewrite it.

### Examples of the standard we're holding

> "…and see what next step, **if any**, makes sense."
> Two words — *if any* — signal we're willing for the answer to be "you're already fine."

> "Most conversations with us don't end in a decision, and that's completely fine."
> Removes pressure instead of manufacturing urgency.

**Corporate solutions** stays discoverable under Solutions. It is a smaller segment and must not compete for a major homepage branch.

### Two arrival states
- **First impression** — no prior familiarity. The site must answer: who is D'Life, what do they do, why credible, which path is mine, what next?
- **Second impression** — arrived via advisor, referral, event, name card, WhatsApp or social. The site must confirm D'Life is real, has depth, and offers a clear way to continue.

> *The first handshake often happens offline. The website confirms trust online.*

# 8. HARD GUARDRAILS — NON-NEGOTIABLE

### Existing-policy journey
This is a service-led trust offering for people who already hold a policy — **not** a client-acquisition play built on dissatisfaction with another advisor.

❌ No insurer or competing agency names
❌ No "lost touch with your agent"
❌ No advisor-abandonment language
❌ No competitor comparison
❌ No agency-switching or client-poaching implication

✓ Clarity · current coverage · guidance · confidence · low-pressure human support

### Recruitment
❌ Mass recruitment, easy-money or lifestyle promises, MLM language, generic motivational claims
✓ Realistic career exploration, mentorship, advisor voices, culture and responsibility

### Founder
Use Sharon as a trust anchor. Never let the site become a personal biography or hero worship.

### Content behaviour
- Important videos play **inside** the D'Life site, with audio
- Visitors never need to leave for social media to finish a story
- Every important video or story leads to related content or a contextual CTA
- Social media feeds the website — the website must not leak serious visitors back out

### Homepage discipline
> **The homepage is a lobby, not a library.**
Each section reveals only enough to create relevance, trust and curiosity, then links deeper. The homepage does not publish the full content of every page beneath it.

### Non-duplication rule
The pathway selector (fast routing) and the life-needs section (emotional exploration) cover similar territory. They must differ in **wording, scale and imagery** so it reads as depth, not repetition.
- Pathway: first person — "Protect my family"
- Life needs: noun-based — "Protecting Family"

# 9. VISUAL SYSTEM

### Typography
- **Lora** — hero headlines, section titles, editorial statements, wordmark. Semibold (600) for headings, Medium (500) for emphasis.
- **Work Sans** — body, navigation, labels, buttons, cards, FAQ, footer.
- Balance: roughly **60% modern / 40% heritage**

### Palette

| Colour | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary teal | `#174A45` | Dark sections |
| Deep green | `#1D4B3E` | Dark sections |
| Dark teal | `#0F332C` | Deepest panels |
| Copper | `#B36B43` | Primary actions |
| Dark copper | `#9C5A37` | Hover |
| Bronze | `#A88752` | Accents |
| Sand | `#D0BFA9` | Warm section backgrounds |
| Cream | `#EFE8DA` | Warm section backgrounds |
| Ivory | `#F6F1E8` | Default page background |
| Charcoal | `#2F3532` | Footer, body text |

### Components
- **Primary action:** copper fill, white text, restrained radius, darker copper hover. One per section, maximum.
- **Secondary action:** outline or text-and-arrow
- **Cards:** warm neutrals, restrained borders, modest rounding, short copy
- **Contact:** contextual CTAs plus one restrained floating WhatsApp point. Never generic, oversized or repeated.
- **Icons:** geometric outline
- **Motion:** quiet reveal as baseline, simple microinteractions, narrative scroll used sparingly, reduced-motion supported

### Layout modes
Story-led split (founder, policy, career) · Pathway modular (routing, life needs) · Open modern (info, FAQ) · Layered depth (hero, leadership, DVA — selective)

### Section rhythm
Light and dark alternate. **No two dark sections adjacent.**

# 13. TECHNICAL & DELIVERY

**Platform:** WordPress

**Reusable modules to build:**
Video card · Story card · Related content · Selected videos · Event card · FAQ preview · Contextual CTA · Stay in the Loop

**Responsive expectations:**
- **Phone** — compact nav, clear hero line breaks, stacked cards, thumb-friendly CTAs, controlled image height, non-obstructive WhatsApp
- **Tablet** — deliberate re-composition, not a shrunken desktop
- **Desktop** — controlled line length, intentional whitespace, useful hover and focus states

**SEO foundation required now:** editable titles and descriptions, descriptive URLs, clean heading structure, alt text, sitemap, indexing readiness, Search Console, analytics access, trackable CTAs, reusable content types. Build these so later upgrades don't require rebuilding.

**Events to track:** WhatsApp clicks by page/CTA · form submissions by enquiry type · video starts and completion · pathway and content performance

**WhatsApp:** one number may be used at launch, but every major CTA carries a distinct pre-filled message so the team sees intent and source before replying.

**Ownership principle:** D'Life must not be permanently dependent on a vendor-owned account for website access, business data, analytics, licences or renewals. Handover must document core access, build register, integrations, and who renews what at what cost.

# 17. THE FIVE THINGS THAT MATTER MOST

1. **Warmth over persuasion.** Every section leads with a human need or a story. Products are never the headline.
2. **Two speeds of empathy.** Fast pills for people who know what they want; emotional life-needs cards for people who don't.
3. **Proof without bragging.** 27 years, real faces, real stories — stated quietly. Understatement reads as confidence.
4. **The generous move.** Helping people understand coverage they bought elsewhere, with zero poaching language, is the strongest trust signal we have.
5. **The contrast pair.** DVA small, dark and closed; Youth Community large, warm and open. Placed together, they explain themselves.
